02-25: Fucking Taiwan

Let me preface this by saying that I loved Taiwan: my ass had never been cleaner, and I felt like I had sucked from the teet of a technologically forward thinking society, and the milk of the United States will forever taste stale.

but, and I cannot stress this enough, fucking taiwan

I had busted my foot up real good about three weeks before the Taiwan trip. As a hopeless romantic, I had tried to apply bouldering rizz to a potentially saucy situation (hinge date), and instead tore a ligament in my ankle and spent the next three weeks zipping around in a scooter with flame decals. I still insist that the flames were critical for me reaching top speeds of 9 m/s (1062.99 barleycorns per second for Americans and non-engineers).

But there was no way I was going to be able to fit my scooter in either carryon or checked baggage, so I prayed and prayed for the weeks leading up to the trip that by some miracle, my ankle would heal. And by some miracle, it healed just enough for me to be able to rawdog being crippled for a week, but not enough for me to not be miserable.

Limping like a champ

The one detail that I've been leaving out up until now is the fact that this was a business trip (to Taiwan and the factories in Vietnam). And not the schmoozing type of business trip--more like the investigating the manufacturing/engineering process kind of business trip that involves walking around factories and waiting three hours for seven factory workers to turn on one machine. I likely spent more time standing and walking than sleeping, which was a massive L.

Apparently, I had this "kicked puppy" type of look about me that imbued everyone with the desire to give me traditional Chinese medicine or foot massages. This "kicked puppy aura", as I have now come to call it, had me dodging non-fda approved treatments left and right, until I finally relented and accepted generic icy hot just to make it stop. The generic icy hot was alright. It didn't bring much physical relief, but the psychic relief the cessation of unsolicited medical advice from not-doctors was priceless.

The Factories

The factories themselves were interesting. I don't want to say too much about specifics, but I will say that some factories utilized a good deal of automation, while others didn't. Thankfully, all of the factories we visited had good working conditions (in terms of A/C, cleanliness, PPE, and regular breaks). These factories resembled some of the factories I've seen in the US, with the number of workers and the type of components being manufactured differentiating the two, at least on a surface level. Even through the conditions were fine, and factories pay better than working a farm, I couldn't help but feel as if I was witnessing something deeply wrong and exploitative. I just had years and years of perceptions and biases about Asian factories dismissed, so what was left to look at was just a factory--with workers assembling PCBAs, applying TIM, and mating plastic enclosures.

But I couldn't explain away this feeling of exploitation. Before me were workers engaging in repetitive and unstimulating work--stripped of their individuality, assuming the function of a robot in a part of the world where machines are worth more than man. They did not talk to each other and they did not look at at us. Their attention was fully occupied with creating a device that some asshole overseas will barely consider, and will only do so if it breaks. The device that they are making is meaningful and their work is important, but they'll never know that. No one will ever tell them that because most people don't consider the work that goes into their products, and most consumers have become accustomed to cheap overseas manufacturing. These workers aren't paid poorly, but they aren't paid well. They're paid "just enough".

And that's what gets me--"just enough". As if being paid "just enough" is adequate compensation for slowly smothering the human spirit through uninspiring and repetitive work with minimal future prospects. As if it's ok to pay a person "just enough" so that the idea of escaping poverty moves into the realm of possibility, but never into the realm of probability.

And the other thing that gets me: what meaningful distinction exists between these Vietnamese factory workers and most of us? It's the same exploitative, treadmill of poverty, chase the dangling carrot bullshit. But we at least have Cheese-Its, Basketball, and the luxury of deluding ourselves into believing that the American Dream hasn't been killed yet by psychopathic billionaires who will gladly drive the world to destruction, if it means getting just one more hit of that sweet sweet green.

But idk man. I'm just some mad asshole online who hates money, is dealing with an abstract version of economic survivors guilt, and a rapidly decreasing tolerance for the normalization abhorent shit.

The Ecological Niche of the "Hypeman"

So I wasn't aware of this before the trip, but "Hypemen" are very important to the Asia business trip ecosystem. I swear to god I can't find any reference to this on the internet, so half of me wonders how much of an ibuprofen induced fever dream this was. From the reconstruction of my recollection, hypemen are dudes that a company will hire (typically under the title of something like business relations), whose sole purpose is to fill us up with platitudes, beer, and delicious food so that we may remember the company favorably when we return to the states. Our resident hypeman didn't just butter us up, he did plan things like transportation and dinners, which I really appreciated.

He was also a funny dude. Absolutely jacked, with the swagger of a teen boy with a backwards baseball cap. Overall 10/10 -- I think thing the workplace jester is an important economical niche that should be protected.

Actually Vietnam

I wish I could remember more about Vietnam, but the jetlag and the extreme desire to exist in a non-gimped body completely memory wiped me. But through the mental fog (and my camera roll), I can see one powerful moment.
Macaron Da Loooong Way

I stared at this small, yet long macaron resting in my hand as the scent of burnt benzene gently caressed my nostril hairs. This fragile creature of confectionary constitution is not long for this cruel world of smoke and smog, but its very presence in my hand was a testament to its incredible willpower.
"Macaron, your length is too long and your circumference too small. You are too weak, you will surely perish. How will you prevail?"
To which, the macaron responded:
"..."
As it was just a macaron.
Unfortunately, long macaron was stale and flavorless -- perhaps the worst macaron that I have ever eaten.
